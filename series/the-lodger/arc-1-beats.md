# Arc 1 — The Lodger (Episodes 1–20)

Format: 60–90 seconds per episode. Every episode ends on a swipe-point. Ep 10 is the paywall cliffhanger. Ending of the series remains open — nothing here forecloses it.

---

**Ep 1 — The Stranger at Dusk.** Rain. Establish her fast: running the boarding house alone, ledger that doesn't balance, father's empty chair. A knock at last light. A young man, beautiful, travel-worn but wrong for it — no mud on him. He asks for a room and pays a month in advance in old gold coins.
*Swipe:* "One condition," he says. "No one enters my room. Not even you." Beat. "Especially you."

**Ep 2 — House Rules.** She shows him up. He requests the room facing away from the sunrise — insists on it. His trunk arrives; it takes four men and the porch groans.
*Swipe:* That night, through the banister rails, she watches him move the trunk across his room. One hand. Silent.

**Ep 3 — Daylight.** The deputy — sunny, broad-shouldered, easy — brings her peaches from his mother's tree, makes her laugh for the first time in the series. He asks, casually, about the new lodger.
*Swipe:* "Just a salesman," she says. She doesn't know why she lied. Neither do we — yet.

**Ep 4 — The Untouched Tray.** She leaves his supper at his door each night; each morning it's untouched. Their first real conversation, at dusk on the stairs. He addresses her by her full name. She never told him her full name.
*Swipe:* Tonight's tray: food untouched — but the little flower she'd set on the napkin is gone.

**Ep 5 — The Ledger.** The banker calls. Foreclosure is coming; her father signed papers she never saw. She keeps her chin up until the door closes, then doesn't.
*Swipe:* At the top of the stairs, in the dark, the lodger has heard every word. His eyes catch the lamplight like an animal's.

**Ep 6 — The Church Social.** The deputy escorts her. Lemonade, fiddle music, an easier life on display — and she lets herself imagine it. She comes home after dark, still humming.
*Swipe:* Vascari is sitting in the unlit parlor, perfectly still. "You were gone long." It shouldn't thrill her. It does.

**Ep 7 — Midnight.** She can't sleep; finds him at the parlor window. He talks about cities she's only seen in books — in the past tense, all of them, like eulogies. She's never been anywhere. "You will be," he says, like a fact.
*Swipe:* Her hand brushes his. Cold as well-water. He pulls back as if *she* burned *him*.

**Ep 8 — The Crest.** A crate arrives, freight-stenciled: LORD V—. The town gets a week of jokes out of it. At the store, a drummer is rude to her; the lodger corrects him with four quiet words and the reflexive authority of a man who has never once been disobeyed.
*Swipe:* The deputy, reading a wire at the station: livestock found dead two counties over. Drained.

**Ep 9 — The Locked Door.** He goes out — only ever at night, she's realized. She stands at his door with her ring of keys. Every key in the house is hers. She reaches for the lock—
*Swipe:* "Were you looking for me?" He's behind her. She never heard the stairs. Outside, thunder — the storm arriving.

**Ep 10 — PAYWALL: The Storm.** The tree comes through the parlor window. Chaos, rain, black glass everywhere. She cuts her palm open — and he is across the room faster than the lightning, her wrist in his hand, and his face is *not his face*. He masters it by inches. "Go upstairs," he whispers. "Bolt your door. Please."
*Swipe:* She bolts it. Stands with her back against it, bleeding, heart hammering. From the other side of the wood, barely audible: "Forgive me."

**Ep 11 — Morning After.** Dawn. His trunk is packed and standing in the hall. He means to vanish — it's what he does. She steps in front of the door. Neither of them can say the true sentence, so they stand in the silence where it should be.
*Swipe:* He carries the trunk back upstairs. Across the street, the deputy watches the lodger's silhouette at her window — and stops smiling for the first time in the series.

**Ep 12 — Inquiries.** The deputy starts pulling threads: wires to Raleigh, to Norfolk. No records. No Vascari anywhere. The lodger paid the store in coin; the deputy borrows one.
*Swipe:* Magnifying glass. Mint date: 1803. Uncirculated. Bright as the day it was struck.

**Ep 13 — The Parlor, Restored.** She wakes to hammering that stops before dawn. The storm-wrecked parlor is rebuilt — perfectly, impossibly, in one night. She catches him finishing as the sky greys, and he sways; the coming light drains him. She gets under his arm and helps him up the stairs. Their longest touch yet, and he lets it happen.
*Swipe:* At his door, eyes half-closed: he says her name — the full name — the way other men say amen.

**Ep 14 — The Father.** Her father comes home loud and mean, waving the foreclosure papers he caused, humiliating her in front of the boarders. She takes it, because she always has.
*Swipe:* Vascari descends the stairs without a sound, leans close to her father, and speaks a single sentence we cannot hear. Her father goes white as the tablecloth — and leaves.

**Ep 15 — Thirty Days.** The bank sets the date. She counts the tin box twice; arithmetic doesn't care. The deputy finds her on the porch and offers his solution, hat in hands: marry him. House saved, life settled, honest and warm.
*Swipe:* She opens her mouth to refuse — and doesn't. Upstairs, a curtain falls back into place.

**Ep 16 — Cold.** Vascari turns to winter overnight — formal, distant, unbearable. She corners him: what is wrong with you? "Nothing. Marry your daylight man." The fight is really a confession and they both know it. "Is that what you want?" she demands.
*Swipe:* He's gone — the room empty between one blink and the next. And this time she *saw* it. Saw him cross to the door faster than anything alive.

**Ep 17 — Old Stories.** She takes what she saw to the oldest woman in town, asking sideways, hypothetical. Granny Holt doesn't answer hypotheticals. She talks instead about a lodger at the Ashworth place, back when *she* was a girl — a beautiful young man who never aged a day.
*Swipe:* "He asked for the room facing away from the sunrise, too."

**Ep 18 — The Mirror.** She hangs a mirror on the landing — an experiment she's ashamed of. That evening he passes it. For one blink the glass shows the lamp, the wallpaper, and no one at all — then he's there again, or was he always?
*Swipe:* The dish in her hands hits the floor. Across the landing, his eyes meet hers, and something in them is very old and very tired: *so now you know to ask.*

**Ep 19 — The Ultimatum, Kindly.** Deadline eve. The deputy presses gently for his answer — and warns her, too: the man has no past, no papers, no reflection in any record. "I can protect you." He means it. That's what makes it worse.
*Swipe:* Nailed to the boarding house door in the morning light: NOTICE OF FORECLOSURE — SALE AT NOON TOMORROW.

**Ep 20 — ARC FINALE: Paid in Full.** Auction day. Boarders whispering, her whole life on the block, the deputy at her side. The banker arrives — flustered. There will be no sale. The debt was settled last night. In full. In gold. Anonymously. She turns to the deputy with tears standing in her eyes — and he smiles, and lets her believe it.
*Swipe:* Upstairs, behind the curtain, Vascari watches her embrace the deputy — and closes his hand around an empty coin purse. We know. She doesn't. **[END ARC 1]**
