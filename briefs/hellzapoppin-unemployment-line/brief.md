## The piece

The Producers close the show: nobody is in the seats, they are all at the talkies, the pictures
talk now. The freight door rolls up and the company walks out of 1929 into 2026. The only place
that will take them is the unemployment office, where a kind, tired counselor tries to enter a
Curtain, a potted palm, a Pierrot and a singer into a system that has a field for none of them.
One of them turns out to have a skill the office needs. The Curtain opens. The Curtain closes.
By the end it hangs across the waiting room, lamp lit at its hem, and whoever gets "placed" goes
through it. The office is the venue. The show was never bankrupt. The Curtain always knew.

## Why it works (the thing to protect)

- The 1941 *Hellzapoppin'* is a vaudeville troupe making a movie because vaudeville is dead. The
  talkies killed it. Our vaudevillians are AI-generated. "Steamrolled by technology" lands twice
  and the piece never has to say so.
- "(2026)" in the title is the joke's year stamp: the line has been moving since 1929. The
  Curtain's queue ticket reads 2026; the display reads "Now serving 1929."
- Every character is correct about something they misunderstand (the Loading Dock rule). The
  counselor is right that none of them has a marketable skill. She is wrong about one of them.

## Shape (~70 s)

### ACT I — THE BANNER AND THE DOCK

**S1 · Banner, torn down (5 s).** The hand-painted canvas banner hangs from two ropes above an
empty row of blue plastic chairs in a fluorescent waiting room: HELLZAPOPPIN' / LIVE! / From The
Unemployment Line, a small painted queue ticket between the lines. A hand in a grey security
sleeve enters, grips a corner and rips it down; the canvas drops out of frame, the ropes swing,
the chairs and the dark display box remain. The display blinks on: *Now serving 1929*. Series
convention: banner first, then action; this episode's transition is the tear-down.

**S2 · The Producers' office (10 s).** Period, warm amber. The shipped pair from No. 4: the stout
balding one in wire glasses and an open vest, the sleek one with pomade and a thin moustache.
Veo-native dialogue. In the doorway behind them, unnoticed: the hem of the Curtain.

**S3 · The freight door (6 s).** From inside the dock, the big roll-up door rises on a 2026
street at dusk: a bus shelter with a glowing ad screen, an electric car on charge, a pedestrian
lit by a phone. The company stands in silhouette against it. One cut does the century.

**S4 · The street (5 s).** The Curtain folded over the curb like a rug put out for collection. The
Palm beside a parking meter, mic stand leaning on it. The Pierrot sitting on her placard. The
Singer holding her mic stand like a cane. A pedestrian steps over the Curtain's hem without
looking down. No dialogue.

### ACT II — THE INTAKE (intercut round-robin, ~30 s)

The counselor's questions are the rhythm. Each answer is its own short shot (2–4 s), over her
shoulder, one character per shot, rotating Curtain, Palm, Pierrot, Singer and back. Each question
is heard once on her reverse; the answers cut against it. The Pierrot never speaks; she answers
with placards. The Singer never speaks; she sings. The Palm is small and papery. The Curtain is
patient, amused, older than authority.

| Round | COUNSELOR | CURTAIN | PALM | PIERROT | SINGER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Name?" | "Curtain." | "Palm." | taps her white cheek | sings a wordless phrase. COUNSELOR: "...I'll put 'Singer.'" |
| 2 | "Last employer?" | "The Loading Dock." | "The Potted Palm." COUNSELOR: "That's... you?" PALM: "It was named after me." | raises HELL | sings "Nooo-body." |
| 3 | "And what did you do there?" | "I opened. I closed." | "I stood near things." | — | begins one long note |
| 4 | "Skills?" | "I have put more people in their seats than you will ever know." | "Four gallons." | raises REMEMBER NOTHING. COUNSELOR: "Detail-oriented." | the note continues. COUNSELOR holds out the receiver: "Can you do that into this?" |
| 5 | "References?" | "They're all still performing." | *(of the plastic ficus on her cabinet)* "Is that one of yours?" COUNSELOR: "It came with the desk." | — | the note, tinny now, from the receiver lying on the desk |
| 6 | "Well." *(to the Curtain)* "We do have one opening." | "I'm familiar with those." | | | |

Rotation need not be strict; a skipped slot is a beat. Trim rounds in the edit, not in the writing.

### ACT III — PLACEMENT (12 s)

**S-final.** The waiting area. The Curtain hangs from a steel rod wall to wall, floor to ceiling,
closing off the waiting room, its face in the upper folds, its brass work lamp lit at the hem, a
laminated badge pinned to the velvet. The Palm, the Pierrot, the Singer and one modern claimant
wait in the chairs in front of it. Off-screen: "Next." The Curtain parts. The Pierrot rises and
walks through with her placard; the velvet closes behind her. A beat. Muffled applause surges
through the velvet and is cut off. The face: the snicker. The lamp stays lit. Black.

## Visual rules

- **Two color temperatures.** The dock is the series' warm amber. The office is cold fluorescent,
  grey carpet, beige walls, blue plastic chairs. The company carries its warmth with it: the
  Curtain brings its own work lamp and plugs it in under her desk. By the end the lamp stands at
  the hem of the partition, which is the series' canonical image relocated.
- **Sparse.** One modern claimant in the row of chairs for scale, no more. Signage illegible; the
  three legible texts (banner, ticket, display) are nano plates or programmatic inserts.
- **Photoreal both eras.** No "vintage" or film-stock words in office prompts (they bake in title
  cards). Technicolor-era language only on dock plates.
- **The Curtain's body.** Seated, locked-off medium shots in the intake; cloth stays legible when
  the camera does not rise. It speaks with fold-mouth shifts, EL voice. As the partition it does
  what Veo does best with curtains: it parts.

## Production notes and risks

- **Banner tear-down (S1).** Anchor A: banner hanging, the hand entering at the corner (implement
  entering, not engaged). The drop is gravity-assisted with a visible mover, which is the kind of
  removal Veo delivers; fallback is the rip in a hard cut with SFX, anchor B = R13 (torn state).
- **Intake coverage.** One OTS anchor per character (R06–R09) doubles as every answer's first
  frame; the counselor's reverse (R03) is one anchor for all questions. Short takes, 4 s, Quality.
- **Identity.** Producers face-ref'd to the shipped No. 4 frame, identity prose verbatim in every
  prompt. Counselor locked from R03; Quality model for every identity-bearing shot.
- **Period subject in a modern plate.** Veo will try to modernize the costume or period-ize the
  office. Anchors carry both; for the Pierrot and the Singer use keyed sprites on plates (the Still
  Juggling method) so the subject is pixel-identical across anchors.
- **Text.** Veo capitalizes and enlarges; sentence case + no-text suppressors; banner, cards, ticket
  and display are plates or inserts. HELL and REMEMBER NOTHING cards shipped in No. 5.
- **Walla.** Office ambience will come back as legible lines; transcribe every take; bury or keep.
- **Singing.** Veo-native singing for the Singer; expect it to add lyrics; the sustained single note
  may need an ElevenLabs rescue layered under a mouth-open hold.
- **RAI.** Benign setting. Avoid "homeless/destitute" clusters in prose. Palm and Curtain are
  objects; Pierrot, Producers, Counselor are photoreal adults.
- **Lamp.** Veo keeps signature lamps lit regardless; the ending uses that.
