## 1. Concept

An old woman on a porch talks to a small device in a language nobody else alive speaks. There are no subtitles: we are locked out with everyone else. The device answers her, haltingly. She corrects it, pleased to have something to correct. As it learns, the subtitles begin to arrive, one word and then a line: the subtitles are the machine's understanding, and ours comes with it. Somewhere else, her granddaughter sits in her room practising with the same kind of device, and the device corrects her the way it was corrected. Then the porch again, the woman alone at dusk, and a voice behind her says hello in her language, badly. She turns, and the film stays on her face. Last, from a distance, the two of them on the porch in the evening light, talking.

The line of transmission is the film: grandmother, device, granddaughter. The machine is not the second speaker. It is how there comes to be one.

## 2. Format

- 1080×1920 (9:16), 24 fps, H.264 high + AAC 48 kHz stereo. Target 42–46 s. The director accepted the extra length for the practice montage and the coda.
- Photoreal, natural light, long-lens stillness. Hard cuts. No music under the dialogue; see section 7.
- Subtitles are part of the picture, set locally. They sit in the middle band of the frame, clear of the Shorts interface zone at the bottom and the right edge.
- Title over the last shot or on a short black tail; decided in the edit.

## 3. Depiction (the director's constraints)

The film must not read as any real people, place or language.

- **No cultural signifiers.** No patterned textiles, jewellery, religious objects, flags, script, signage, regional architecture, food or craft. Clothing is plain and contemporary: a cardigan, a cotton shirt, a jumper.
- **An unplaceable setting.** A weathered timber porch on an ordinary house, a chair, a small table, a field and a line of trees beyond. Temperate, late summer. Nothing that names a country.
- **Not Native American, and not a stand-in for any endangered-language community.** The woman is not presented as an elder of a people, a keeper of tradition or a subject of study. She is somebody's grandmother, and the language is simply hers. No ceremony, no elegy, no archival framing, no researcher.
- **Casting described by person, not by type.** Two members of one family with a visible resemblance; ordinary faces; no costume. The casting choice is an open decision for the director (status page).
- **She has the authority.** She teaches; the device and the girl learn. Nothing in the film is done to her or for her. She is funny, and a little exacting.
- **The device is not a brand and not a screen.** A small cloth-covered puck with one soft light that breathes when it speaks. No logo, no interface, no phone.

## 4. The language

Invented for this film. It has to sound like a real, lived-in language while resembling no particular one.

- **Sound.** Open, even syllables; consonants `m n l r s v d t k h`; five plain vowels. No clicks, ejectives, glottal stops, tones, retroflexes or long consonant clusters; nothing that imitates the sound of a real language family. Words of two or three syllables. The stress falls late, which is what the device gets wrong.
- **Checked before lock.** Each word was searched for accidental meaning in real languages on 2026-11-22. Four of the first six were replaced: one was the self-name of a people in Nepal, one the name of a people and language in Mozambique, one read as ordinary Hawaiian, and the taught word sounded like the Italian for "sister". A first replacement greeting was dropped because it is a word in two Arctic and Tungusic languages. The words below have only incidental neighbours (a given name, a Slovak slang goodbye, an Estonian case form, a Finnish word for joy). The check is a search, not a proof; any word is replaced on a credible report.
- **Small and consistent.** The film needs six words. The same word is always pronounced the same way by all three voices, apart from the two scripted mistakes.

Lexicon v2:

| Word | Said | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| noréla | no-RÉ-la | the light before evening |
| lena doví | LE-na do-VÍ | say it again |
| suvé | su-VÉ | hello |
| ilo | i-LÓ | grandmother |
| vea | VÉ-a | yes; good |
| voké ru | vo-KÉ ru | not like that |

Three more words were added on 2026-11-22 for the one full sentence in the film, chosen and checked the same way: *tové* (comes), *seli* (early), *ené* (now). The sentence is **Noréla tové seli ené**: "The evening light comes early now."

## 6. Agent's choices

- **The subtitles are the arc.** Nothing translated until the machine has it right; partial lines shown with gaps where it does not know the word yet. This is the idea the director singled out and everything else is arranged to protect it.
- **She does not answer.** The director's call: B4 ends on her face. "Say it again" stays in B2, where it is the second thing ever subtitled.
- **The coda is not subtitled.** Mine: the film opens with us shut out of the language and closes with us outside it again, by choice. The word for the light may be just audible.
- **The device corrects the girl with the grandmother's stress.** Mine: the same mistake travels down the line and is fixed the same way, so B3 is not only a montage of effort but the hand-off itself.
- **The device is dark in B4.** It is on the table and it is not needed.
- **The first word taught is a word for light.** Something she can point at, with no cultural freight, that the picture can show.

## 7. Sound

Every voice is ElevenLabs, dubbed over the picture (the director's decision); nothing spoken by the motion model reaches the mix.

- **Casting by ear.** Designed voice candidates for the grandmother, the granddaughter and the device are auditioned alongside the casting stills, each reading lines from the lexicon.
- **On-camera lines.** The take is generated with the performer speaking the scripted syllables so that mouth, breath and timing exist in the picture; that guide performance is then converted to the cast voice by speech-to-speech, which keeps the timing. This is the method used for the Reptile Commander in No Enemy. Where the guide drifts from the script, the line is voiced from text and laid to the mouth instead.
- **The device** is voice-over from text, placed in the room with a small speaker treatment. Its two readings of the taught word (wrong, then right) are made first and are the pronunciation reference for everyone else.
- **The coda** is two voices from text, low and far off, under the field. The shot is wide enough that mouths are not readable.
- Beds: field air, insects, a board creaking; the girl's room is quieter and closer. No score under speech. One held tone may enter on the turn in B4 and carry through the coda and title, if the cut wants it.

## 8. Risks

- **Guide speech drifting from the script.** Because nobody knows the language, small drift is free, but the taught word must match across voices. The device's reading is the reference; the fallback is a text-voiced line on a shot where her mouth is not the subject.
- **Dub sync on the close shots.** B2 is the tightest. Keep her lines short, and cut to the device or the field on anything that will not sit.
- **The last close-up carries B4.** If the turn is not there, the alternative is to hold on her back and let the girl's face answer.

## 9. Casting

- The granddaughter is about twenty, home for a visit (decided 2026-11-22). No minors are generated.
- Three candidate pairs go to the director as reference stills with voice options. One pair, at the director's request, has the feel of a remote Balkan or Macedonian upland: faces, light and a stone-and-timber house, with the rule on signifiers unchanged (no costume, embroidery, icons, script or flags).
