### Continuity and execution

- Exactly two adult characters. The knight wears a plain dark tunic and mail;
  Death has an ordinary human face in pale stage make-up and a matte black cowl.
  Original casting and staging, without reproducing the source film's actors.
- The knight remains screen-left facing right; Death remains screen-right facing
  left. Keep the horizon, low board and two stools fixed in the returning wides.
  An intermediate aged profile makes the unchanged earbud legible.
- Never show a chess position close enough to demand a legal move. No elaborate
  piece transfers. The earbud stays in place; the phone is already in the hand
  when revealed. Make the departures an ellipsis, starting the last shot in motion.
- Establish identity in scene, inspect anchors before motion, and render time
  changes as separate takes. Tide changes are between shots, never a simulated
  timelapse of faces. Close views carry the beard change if the wide is too small.
- Target native 1080 × 1920 review. Local edit, monochrome grade, title and final
  sound assembly. Inspect motion and dialogue separately from technical checks.

## Original concept — recorded 2026-10-19; historical planning status

Developed in a concept conversation with the director (trope subversions with an AI turn) and
set aside in favour of *Balcony* for the October 2026 slot. Kept here so it is not lost.

**Logline.** Death plays chess with a knight on a grey beach for his life. The knight has an
engine in his ear. Death, after a long look, produces his own. Every game is a draw.

**The trope.** *The Seventh Seal*: the hooded figure and the knight at a board on the shore,
playing for a soul. It reads from a single frame, and it has been parodied widely enough that a
general audience understands "a game with Death for your life" without knowing the source.

**The turn.** Neither side can win once both consult the oracle. The tide comes in and goes
out, the knight's beard greys, Death does not change, and the game cannot end, so neither can
the knight. Death, finally: "Shall we call it?" The two of them walk the shoreline together like
old colleagues. The board is left to the water.

**The title.** *Perpetual check* is the chess term for a forced draw by endless checks: the king
is never mated and never safe. Death keeps checking. The game cannot end, and neither can he.

**What it says.** When both sides have the oracle the contest stops resolving and starts
meaning nothing; outplaying death is not the same as living. Dry, not loud. The joke is one
image (a glow under the cowl) and one line.

## Shape (sketch, about 40 to 50 seconds, near-wordless)

1. Beach, wide, black and white. Death and the knight at the board. The classic frame, held.
2. Close on the knight. A modern white earbud under the mail coif; a faint voice gives a move.
   He plays it, with a small private smile.
3. Death's hand hovers over the board and stops. Under the cowl, a glow: a phone. Death plays
   the engine's reply without comment.
4. The draws. Pieces reset; the tide in; the tide out; the knight's beard greying; Death the
   same. Board positions are never shown close (see build notes).
5. Death, quietly: "Shall we call it?" Or wordless: Death extends a hand and the knight takes it.
6. The two walk the water's edge together, away from camera. The board on the sand; a wave
   reaches it. Title.

Alternative turn considered and set aside: Death proposes other games and each one has
already been solved (checkers, Go, poker, and so on). A list gag; the stalemate is the image.

## Build notes for whoever picks it up

- Black-and-white photoreal in the Bergman register: hard overcast light, wind, surf as the bed.
  At most one or two spoken lines. If Death speaks, Veo native audio for the on-camera line.
- Chess boards are a known weak point of the video generator (pieces merge and move
  illegally). Keep the board small in wides, never show a legal position close, and build any
  board insert as a still with programmatic motion rather than a generated take.
- Death's white face is stage make-up on a human-shaped figure, not a skull, for the content
  filter and for taste. Keep the figure's hands visible when it plays.
- The glow under the cowl is the one insert that must be close, and the one laugh. Protect it.
- Time passing is best done as hard cuts on the locked wide (tide, light, beard), not as
  dissolves; each state is its own anchor from the same plate.
- Format undecided; 9:16 is likely for the channel, and the vertical frame suits the board
  between two seated figures with the sea above them.
