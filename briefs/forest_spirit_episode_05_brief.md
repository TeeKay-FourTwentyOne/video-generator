## Directorial intent

One magical discovery, one comic escalation, one unsettling final image.

Paper receives a map whose folds affect the forest. Wood and Clay experience
the consequences without knowing their friend is responsible. Paper eventually
recognizes them on the map, giving him a meaningful way to help. Then something
larger starts following their miniature footprints.

Let Episode 04's warmth shade into unease. The forest should feel independently
alive, not suddenly become a different genre. No gore, jump-scare sting, monster
reveal or explanation of the entire world. Let the final image do the work.

## Continuity and character decisions

- Paper is still at the watermill. Wood and Clay remain several hours away.
  A moving map is not a portal; do not compress their journey or reunite them.
- Resolve Paper's bridge duty immediately: show the workers' replacement plank
  taking the load, then Paper withdrawing and standing. Do not simply reset him
  upright or spend another episode transporting workers across his body.
- The foreman gives the map as thanks. Keep him brisk and matter-of-fact, not
  a second Spirit launching another story-within-a-story.
- Paper is curious and helpful. The first fold is an innocent attempt to handle
  an awkward map, not a deliberate prank on his friends.
- Clay notices the sign move and stops Wood from following it blindly. Give him
  observational competence; no repeat root trip or automatic humiliation.
- Wood supplies confident interpretation and fragile dignity. His certainty,
  not a lack of intelligence, is the joke.
- Preserve wood, clay, folded paper and felt/twine construction. Paper's printed
  face stays printed; acting comes from pose, hands, framing and holds.
- Keep the established voices: Wood/Luke, Clay/Baxter, Paper/Elowen and
  Foreman/George. No new speaking character is needed.

## The map: rules to demonstrate, not explain

For this episode, demonstrate only a local correspondence: folding a marked
junction on the map rotates its matching wooden sign in the forest. The map
also shows the travelers moving along the trail. Do not establish that Paper
can create exits, teleport people or reshape the whole landscape at will.

Use the same fork geometry and a recognizable crooked post on paper and in
the forest. Keep camera orientation consistent. Show the finger contacting the
corner, the crease changing and the real sign turning, in that order. A brief
pause in the fold should correspond to a pause in the sign's movement.

The map must also be legible as a physical object: rough paper, crease shadow,
inked paths, tactile edges and held poses. It is distinct from Paper's body.
Use recognizable tall-hatted Wood and round Clay symbols, rather than two
anonymous dots requiring dialogue to identify them.

The final footprints are autonomous. Paper's hands are visibly still or clear
of the map before they appear. They follow the two travelers, not the mill.
Keep their owner unidentified; do not quietly decide that it is Spirit or a
particular monster. That is a later story choice.

## Provisional structure — 56 seconds

Target 45–60 seconds, vertical 9:16. Working animatic target: 56 seconds,
1,344 frames at 24 fps. These are editorial envelopes, not synthetic speech
deadlines. Prefer roughly 12–16 shots, with room to register physical changes.

| Time | Picture and performance | Sound / editorial purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | Open out of black onto the last repair pin going in. The replacement plank bears the load; Paper folds back upright beside it. | Mill rhythm and one small wooden knock. Resolve the previous predicament without recap. |
| 5–10 s | The foreman presents a folded map from a spool-sized rack. Paper takes it carefully. Establish the map separately from his body. | Foreman: “For your trouble.” Paper: “A map?” Dry, unceremonious handoff. |
| 10–16 s | Map insert: a familiar mill, a winding route and the crooked sign at a fork. Paper lifts and bends the corner containing that junction. | Paper rustle begins; carry its rhythm into the sign's creak. Do not cut away before contact reads. |
| 16–24 s | Wood and Clay approach the matching sign with genuine forward travel. They stop; the sign keeps turning. Clay catches the movement and raises a hand to stop Wood. | Clay: “That moved.” Wood: “Wind.” Allow a silent look at the otherwise still foliage. |
| 24–31 s | Back at the mill, Paper asks about the map. Cut mid-thought to Wood inspecting the arrow, now aimed squarely into an impassable trunk. | Paper: “And this will get them—” / Wood: “—absolutely nowhere.” The second line belongs naturally to Wood's immediate problem. |
| 31–38 s | Paper eases the fold open. Match to the sign returning toward the usable path. Clay waits for it to settle before moving; Wood straightens his hat as if this was the plan. | A reverse crease/creak motif. No spoken explanation of the magic and no second verbal punchline. |
| 38–45 s | Two tiny figures move on the map. Briefly match their spacing and direction to the real Wood and Clay proceeding down the path; return to Paper recognizing them. | Paper: “There you are.” This is discovery and affection, not exposition. |
| 45–51 s | Paper holds the map flat and stops touching its folds. Behind the two figures, one oversized footprint appears, then a second closer to their route. | Music thins out. One soft, heavy contact sound with the second print; avoid a loud scare cue. |
| 51–56 s | Paper's hands remain still. A third print lands nearer the travelers. Push into its rounded heel, composed on the center axis, and iris close around it. | Let the audience notice before cutting. No “Who's that?” or foreman explanation. Preserve a short final black tail. |

The arrow pointing into a trunk is the immediate reason for Wood's
“absolutely nowhere”; do not imply an unseen completed loop through the forest.
There is no extra giant unfolding-map gag, chase, new location or flashback.
If the cut runs long, shorten the handoff and hat adjustment before sacrificing
the map-to-sign proof or the time needed to understand the approaching prints.

## Visual and sound language

- Separate the locations with warm mill light and cooler forest light. Match
  shapes and screen direction across the cut, not identical color grades.
- Favor a locked or nearly locked camera for the magic. A stable trunk and
  post make the sign's rotation unambiguous. Background travel belongs in the
  approach shot, not in a stationary reaction shot.
- Keep the sign hinge fixed and its arrow visibly attached. Use a contact
  shadow and changing foreshortening, not a flat horizontal flip.
- Use discrete poses and purposeful holds. Do not add constant camera wobble
  to imply stop motion. If the camera moves, move the whole scene coherently.
- Design the final print now: a broad, readable silhouette with a rounded heel
  that can anchor the centered iris. Keep it off the two character symbols so
  the image suggests pursuit, not that someone has already been crushed.
- Reuse the mill pulse and restrained forest ambience. The paper-rustle / wood-
  creak sound bridge should connect the action without implying the travelers
  can hear Paper from hours away.
- Leave the last several seconds largely wordless. No explanatory caption,
  sting, extra quip or cut back to a routine reaction after the final reveal.

## Acceptance checklist

- The opening visibly transfers the load off Paper; no unexplained reset.
- Wood and Clay are still remote from the mill. No accidental reunion or portal.
- Map junction, sign and screen direction correspond across every cut.
- The sign moves relative to a stable post and environment; Clay notices first.
- The split sentence is clear as both a verbal connection and a local reaction.
- Paper recognizes the two moving figures without a lengthy explanatory line.
- Footprints approach from behind, remain distinct from the figures' own tracks,
  and appear independently of Paper's hands. The last print reads on a phone.
- The final print and iris share one center. No residual caption or extra gag
  obscures the closing image; deliberate final black is distinguished from errors.
- No treadmill walk, frozen top strip, blanket camera wobble, unintended clones,
  incorrect captions, clipped dialogue or accidental shot-boundary flashes.
- Full decode, frame count, duration, caption bounds, dialogue timing and audio
  levels pass. Technical measurements are not described as direct listening.
