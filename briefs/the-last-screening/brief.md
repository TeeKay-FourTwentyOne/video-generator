A deserted Art Deco movie palace lies underwater. A single fish swims down its aisle. An old projector wakes, a small school gathers, and a sunlit meadow plays on the screen. A close view catches warm light on one fish before the film returns to the wide auditorium.

The tone is mysterious, tender and quietly hopeful. Photoreal cobalt water, faded burgundy velvet, tarnished brass and warm projected sunlight establish the visual language. A restrained original chamber waltz accompanies native projector and underwater sound. There is no narration or dialogue. The title appears over the final shot.

## Directing feedback for future films

The finished film was approved. Future pieces should take stronger directorial
control through deliberate shot size, movement, mood and emphasis. In this film,
one or two tighter close-ups of a fish's face could have made its response to
the projected sunlight more expressive. Plan such close-ups and reaction beats
alongside the establishing compositions, and make important motions easy to
read. This guidance is incorporated into the production workflow.
