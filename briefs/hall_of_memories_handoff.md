## Concept notes that matter during implementation

- The cold open must first feel like the actual film. No narrator, frame-story
  label or obvious imaginary-world cue at its start. First establish the bored
  worker's routine. Bring in the storyteller as the premise starts to strain.
- Every factory discontinuity answers a specific child's objection. The second
  worker, vanishing door, sudden timer and timer's eyes are authored events.
  The contradiction "Then why did they need the man?" is the key reversal.
- The robot takeover is a child's legend. Its cheerful robot overseers do not
  establish the actual history or moral character of the polis residents.
- Give the first human record space to change the emotional register after the
  factory argument. The Hall conveys the weight of lived experience. Avoid
  turning the scene into a moral scorecard or declaring humanity deserved its
  fate. The elder's interpretation can remain partial.
- Choosing not to have children is only one suggested way to imply the gradual
  disappearance. It has not been established as the literal history. The elder
  being a former biological human is also an open choice. Digital children may
  be later descendants, not uploaded biological children.
- Granular records of billions of individuals need not include every final
  survivor. Preserve the unknown last death. The Hall's record coverage and the
  community's knowledge can be incomplete in different ways.
- The human recurrence is a speculative premise. Do not invent alien seeding,
  surviving human colonies or a polis cloning program to explain it. Geological
  and evolutionary time must feel vast without asserting unchosen dates.
- The aliens find a surface that appears empty of inhabitants. Plants and
  jungles are present; no large animals are visible during their visit. Later
  animal evolution belongs after the ship leaves.
- Keep the daughter's adulthood and her initial unhappiness legible. Her
  fascination with the jewel is its own reaction. The later scene implies the
  union through staging and sound, with intimate activity outside the image.
- Final crowd: complete physical stillness. No habitual blink, head turn,
  breathing loop or procedural fidget. Concern comes from collective gaze,
  composition, duration and sound.
- Preserve the exact repeated line "Where is everybody?" The alien speaks a
  fictional language with that English subtitle; the human feast has no
  dialogue subtitles. Do not automatically import Scary Woods' character-label
  captions into this film. Decide any wider caption treatment separately.
- Preserve the end: party -> identifiable infant cry -> the Hall's screaming,
  black hold, crescendo, then 0.25 seconds of silence. The infant is the daughter
  and warrior's child, about nine months later. The final silence ends the film;
  any credits placement must respect it.

## Local implementation approach

use persistent 3D geometry where changing camera position or scale demands it.
Choose the simplest technique that sells each shot. A general-purpose character
rigging system is not a prerequisite for beginning the animatic.

## Sound, review and spending

Develop sound alongside picture. Retain separate dialogue, individual Hall
voices, growing scream layers, factory machinery, party, infant, ambience and
any music. Use fictional performances or appropriately sourced effects for
the archive. A composite's density can create escalation without making painful
volume the effect. Reuse recognizable Hall material at the end.

Review the final passage by listening as well as measuring. At 24 fps the final
quarter-second is six frames; at 48 kHz it is 12,000 samples. Once delivery
settings are chosen, verify the intended interval in the decoded export,
including reverb tails and codec padding. Black frames and a motionless crowd
are expected here; generic black/freeze detection needs exact cue context.

Likewise, QA must distinguish the specifically scripted duplicate worker and
factory set changes from unintended clones or drift. Record the intended event
and frame range, then review it visually. Do not exempt all factory footage
from inspection. Generated motion, if used, still follows
`.claude/rules/clip-acceptance-gate.md` with scene-specific context.

Before paid production, establish the active chapter's scope and ceiling and a
new ledger. The $20 floor is not a ceiling or authority to multiply spending by
the proposed chapter count. Include imagery, voices, any generated motion, QA
and contingency. Charge shared assets once and record which chapters reuse
them. Recheck available tools and prices at that point. Keep allocations,
reservations, known charges and unmetered usage distinct; image call counts do
not establish a bill or an exact model identity.

Recommended review delivery is native resolution with a smaller review copy,
the complete rough cut, ledger summary and notes on unresolved creative issues.
Leave upscaling until the edit is selected. Preserve approved versions, sources,
recipes and evidence. Publication and source release follow their separate
repository checkpoints; any later commit/push needs `docs/source-privacy.md`.
