## Proposed Exchange segment

Begin in a cave or primitive-looking hall during a rowdy victory feast.
Establish three clearly distinct adults: the victorious warrior, the chief,
and the chief's adult daughter. The warrior demands her in marriage as a war
prize. Both she and the chief are unhappy. He then produces the same jewel
that houses the polis, and her attention becomes absorbed by it.

Recommended coverage, with timing to be found through blocking:

| Beat | What must read | Initial coverage idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Victory feast | The warrior has standing and an audience; locate the chief and daughter | One economical ensemble view with controlled background action |
| Claim | The warrior claims a reward, then indicates the daughter | Medium coverage that shows the relationship between his gestures and their targets |
| Resistance | The chief opposes the demand; the daughter withdraws | Separate reactions with enough time to register unhappiness |
| Reveal | The warrior introduces the familiar jewel as leverage | Hand/prop insert tied clearly to his preceding action |
| Fascination | The daughter attends to the jewel despite her unhappiness about the demand | Held reaction and a matching jewel view; stop on her absorbed attention |

No dialogue subtitles or explanatory title cards. Any vocal sounds should
support the social scene; the exchange must be intelligible from acting and
editing. A busy wide alone is unlikely to carry the demand. Her fascination
must remain a separate beat from consent: no approving smile, reciprocal
romantic gesture or edit that silently changes the meaning of her resistance.

The recommended boundary is the jewel's hold on her attention. The later-night
scene on the furs, necklace, polis witnesses and final sound sequence are a
separate production unit. The evolution/retrieval bridge also remains separate.
Do not append this scene directly to the ship's departure and imply that the
missing recurrence passage has been completed.
