# POLISH

9:16 · Proposed 38 seconds within the 30–40-second target · Working title

Treatment and framing revised 2026-11-15 from the director's notes. The exchange
below is the published 38-second held-phone revision, confirmed by the director.
[Original pitch](pitch-v1.md) · [Planning status](status.md).

A woman messages someone she trusts. Her phone starts by trying to complete her
word “terrible” as “terrific.” She types past the prediction and sends what she
wrote. A later suggestion offers friendlier phrasing; it seems reasonable, so she
takes it. Only when she tries to say she needs help does the
assistance become compulsory: her message cannot send until she accepts its
positive replacement. A final thumbs-up reassures the person on the other end.

The turn is from choice to habit to coercion. Her first refusal genuinely works;
her first acceptance is voluntary. The interface retains the same calm appearance
throughout. Her correspondent is attentive and responds to what actually arrives.

## Proposed exchange

The conversation is already open on a short exchange from yesterday:
**“Did you get home?” / “Yep. Thanks for tonight.” / “Anytime.”** It is visible
before the new **“How was today?”** arrives under a Today divider, and scrolls
away naturally as the current conversation grows. These ordinary earlier messages
establish an ongoing relationship without explaining the plot.

| Beat | Time | Conversation and action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S01 — Optional | 0–7 s | Incoming: **“How was today?”** She types **“Pretty ter…”** toward **“terrible.”** Inline prediction supplies **“terrific”** and keeps offering it as she finishes the word. She types past it and sends **“Pretty terrible.”** Her exact wording becomes a delivered bubble. |
| S02 — Helpful | 7–14 s | Incoming: **“Want to talk?”** She types **“Maybe later.”** Suggested: **“In a little while?”** She accepts the softer wording and sends it. This is a small, believable concession. |
| S03 — Required | 14–24 s | Incoming: **“You okay though?”** She types **“Not really. I need you.”** Suggested: **“I’m fine.”** She tries Send. The text stays in the composer; a small helper reads **“Apply suggestion to send.”** Send is disabled. A second tap changes nothing. A quiet offscreen **“No.”** |
| S04 — Surrender | 24–33 s | She clears the unsent draft and types **“Please stop.”** The suggestion is **👍**. Send remains disabled. **“Oh, come on.”** Another futile tap, then a tired exhale. Her offscreen touch accepts the suggestion; only now does Send enable. She taps Send and the thumbs-up becomes a delivered bubble. |
| S05 — Reassured | 33–38 s | A brief typing indicator, then the notification and **“Good. I was worried about you.”** Hold on the exchange for about four seconds. The input remains open; she types nothing. |

For S01, distinguish her solid typed letters from the pale predicted suffix:
**Pretty ter** + pale **rific**, then **Pretty terr** + pale **ific**, then
**Pretty terri** + pale **fic**. A standard keyboard prediction candidate also
offers **terrific**. As soon as she types **b**, the incompatible ghost suffix
disappears; the **terrific** candidate persists above the keyboard while she
finishes **terrible**. Send remains enabled. She ignores the candidate and sends
**“Pretty terrible.”** The ellipsis marks a pause in the treatment, not literal
punctuation she types. This first intervention stays at the level of a single
word completion; S02 introduces the broader phrasing suggestion. In the current
framing the candidate remains visible below the composer; the keyboard itself
is below the camera frame throughout.

Only sent messages enter the conversation history. Both rejected drafts remain
visible long enough to read before she clears or replaces them. The first two
outgoing messages reach the other person normally. At the coercive turn, accepting
the suggested text and sending are distinct, visible actions. It never sends a
message without her final tap.

The terrible/terrific opening, progression and physical-screen direction come
from the director. The remaining exchange, 38-second allocation, helper wording,
precise prediction behavior and insert placements are the agent's proposal.
The original thumbs-up and final reply are retained; their
mechanism changes from automatic rewriting/sending to forced acceptance.

## Picture

A familiar, fictional phone messaging interface: ordinary bubbles, timestamps,
blinking caret, pale inline completion, word prediction row, later phrasing
suggestion chip, dismiss control and Send. The keyboard remains outside the shot.
Use an original arrangement and restrained colors without copying a particular
messenger, its logos or distinctive graphics. Completion and suggestion controls
share the same understated visual style. A small tonal highlight replaces the
pitch's decorative sparkle.

The main view is close and oblique. The handset extends below the portrait frame,
as though held by a woman immediately offscreen. Her fingers and the keyboard
stay out of view. The conversation, input, prediction, later recommendation and
Send control remain readable. Small grip changes and movement at actual typing
and contact times imply her physical presence. The handset moves independently
of a soft, dark room background; there is no visible tabletop or contact shadow.
The screen retains its glass and subtle reflections without interfering with text.

Keep the device, display texture, type, message history and input state continuous

carry that same artwork into the physical screen views so lettering and spelling
remain precise. At the macro scale, use a consistent display-pixel structure and
optical treatment. The main view stays clean enough for immediate reading.

## Sound and performance

One adult woman close to the phone, tired and increasingly irritated. Her short
offscreen comments and breath are performance, not narration: the proposed lines
are **“No.”** and **“Oh, come on.”**, followed by the exhale before accepting the
thumbs-up. Keep them conversational, quiet and unperformed; she never reads the
messages aloud. Adjust their placement around text-reading time.

Typing has uneven human rhythm. Pause briefly at **“Pretty ter”** so the predicted
**“terrific”** registers, then let her finish **“terrible”** with slightly firmer
taps. Later, a small correction and a faster retype register her impatience.
Soft key taps follow actual characters; fingertip
contacts mark Send attempts. Incoming messages have one consistent notification
sound. A very small standard input cue can mark an accepted suggestion. Failed
Send attempts get no dramatic error sting. Low room tone carries the pauses;
there is no score or synthetic assistant voice. After the last notification,
leave the hold free of further commentary.
