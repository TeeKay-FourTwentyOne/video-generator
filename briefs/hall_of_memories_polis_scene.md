## Dramatic direction

Three children present different ways of dealing with uncertainty. The
storyteller wants a satisfying ending. The challenger wants the story to make
sense. The quiet child starts playfully, then imagines the loneliness of the
last person. That changes the scene's question from how the story works to
where everybody has gone. The older resident recognizes the question.

No one supplies a definitive account. The elder's “I used to ask that, too”
does not establish a former biological identity or explain software childhood.

## Draft dialogue

**Storyteller:** Will you let me finish?

**Challenger:** You did finish. There was nobody left.

**Storyteller:** That's how it ends.

**Challenger:** Except for the timer.

**Quiet child:** Maybe the timer told the story.

**Storyteller:** Timers don't tell stories.

**Challenger:** Yours had eyes.

The storyteller holds the objection for a beat, then offers the story back.

**Storyteller:** Fine. You tell it.

**Challenger:** I don't know what happened.

**Storyteller:** Then mine might be right.

**Quiet child:** Was there really a last one?

**Challenger:** There must have been.

**Quiet child:** Just one person. With nobody to talk to?

Let the question sit. Open the view to the quiet polis.

**Quiet child:** Where is everybody?

The older resident approaches from the side. The children turn toward them.

**Older resident:** I used to ask that, too.

Hold briefly on the new conversation, then end this segment.
