# RED TEARS

Published on [YouTube](https://youtube.com/shorts/2sQ_MACZU_k) on 2026-10-24.
The released v5 4K master is 2160 × 3840, 24 fps and 302 frames
(12.583 seconds). Its closing flash uses the director-selected **05 refined**
voice audition. The director accepted the delivered film before publication.
See the [current production record](README.md) and [published description](description.md).

The director selected RED TEARS and asked for a simpler version while retaining
the severe visual style. This is the current treatment; the initial alternatives
remain in [the first workshop](workshop-v1.md).

## Intent

A 12.58-second, 9:16 horror short: one red jewel liquefies into a blood streak,
flows upward into the empty eye socket above it, and wakes a human eye.
A thin wet stain remains on the cheek. The mask remains rigid. Its cold beauty
makes the small living detail frightening.

The jewel's softening is a brief change of texture. The liquid stretches while
its tail clings to the cheek; uneven thickness and wet highlights make the
upward movement tactile. The suddenly open eye supplies the shock. The other
jewels, painted tears and far socket stay unchanged. The main shot has one active eye and one rivulet. After the cut to black, a
three-quarter-second frontal flash reveals both eyes, frightened behind the mask.

The latch and enclosed air imply that opening a sealed object has awakened it.
The image supplies the story without dialogue, an opening title or a visible
room. Exact sound placement will follow the usable motion rather than an
assumed frame-perfect provider response.

## Visual design

Preserve the supplied mask's white face, asymmetric black eye decoration,
long painted tears, thin red eye accents, red jewels and fixed red lips.
Use cold hard grazing light, sharp lacquer reflections, deep sockets and
saturated ruby red. Let the far side disappear into black.

The opening keyframe is a close three-quarter portrait. The active socket is
on the viewer's left. The target is the isolated red jewel immediately below
the inner row of three jewels, near the nose. Its path must remain short and
visible on a phone. The blood keeps contact with the cheek. The eye has real
moisture, lids and a dark pupil, with only enough surrounding skin to sit
plausibly behind the shell.

The original square image and new portrait keyframe are preserved privately.
The keyframe closely retains the design while changing composition and
lighting; it is not a pixel-identical extraction of the original.

## Production approach

Native review: 1080 × 1920, 24 fps, 302 frames. An eight-second local composite
covers the transformation and final stare. It uses the second generated take's
clean mask plate, ruby and near-eye performance. Local compositing supplies the
blood's changing shape, depth, highlights and retained film. The shell stays
fixed while the blood follows a controlled upward path. The eye opens in roughly
one third of a second. The opening hold matches the composite's actual first
frame. The original black hold is shortened by six frames. Eighteen frames
of a separate frontal take follow, then two black frames end playback.

The soundtrack uses original local synthesis: latch, glass friction, jewel tick,
sticky liquid pull, eye scrape, quiet enclosure pressure and breath-shaped air. Each sound remains
an editable stem. The original cut uses these local stems. The coda now adds a dedicated ElevenLabs nonverbal voice effect, shaped into
a brief muffled cry with enough vocal presence to read during the flash. The earlier
soundtrack retains its original timing and level. The director accepted the final
mix. Agent real-time listening and a specific phone-speaker check are not claimed.

Inspect whether only the specified jewel moves, its upward path remains
readable, and only the near socket gains an eye. Also inspect mask drift,
fluid duplication, unwanted camera moves and late-take escalation. If the
continuous transfer fails, inspect the actual frames before deciding whether
a cut at the socket rim can preserve the event.

## Production history

The following records the successive review stages. Standard archive retention
keeps the final v5 4K master, original takes, reusable shot assets, all voice
auditions, stems, recipes and QA. Earlier complete edits and derived full mixes
are removed after archive verification; their delivery records and hashes remain.

The original image, portrait and frontal anchors, all generated takes, local composite
versions, original sound stems and exact edit recipe are preserved privately.
Image creation records retain the prompts and reference hashes; the built-in
tool did not expose its model name or seed. Motion creation records retain the
submitted controls and source hashes.

The first generated take moved the tear downward and revealed two eyes. The
second supplied a usable single-eye performance but did not move the bead up
the cheek. Local compositing supplies that movement. A pale edge found in the
first composite was corrected after close frame inspection.

The director found that the first review's moving bead read as a sliding circle
and requested a clearer, more tactile blood streak. The revision replaces that
translation with a connected rivulet, a shrinking base pool, changing thickness,
moving highlights and a thin retained stain. The eye timing and 12-second edit
remain the same. Dense flow frames and automated anomaly/duplication checks
have been inspected.

The director approved native v2 and requested a 4K upscale. The delivered
2160 × 3840 finish preserves its 288 frames, 24 fps timing and copied soundtrack.
The local 2× Lanczos finish adds no creative changes. All frame timestamps,
audio packets and decoded audio match the approved cut; colour tags and
full-size blood, eye and lacquer crops were checked. Native versions were retained
through review. Agent real-time listening is not claimed.

The director then requested the frontal coda. Its selected first eighteen source
frames show both eyes widening and a slight nervous movement. The mask stays
rigid and the left-side red residue remains visible. Later reported residue
variation is outside the selected range. The original cut and its earlier 4K
master were retained through review. The coda was delivered as a separate revision.

The revised native and 4K exports contain 302 frames at 24 fps. All 282 retained
native prefix frames match the approved version pixel for pixel. The earlier
soundtrack is unchanged before the new cue, with its original tail preserved
under the flash. The 4K finish preserves all frame timestamps and AAC packets
from the corrected native revision. Later voice revisions retain this picture.

The director found the closing voice insufficiently audible and requested
ElevenLabs. The revised sound uses a dedicated, strongly voiced muffled cry,
locally fitted to the same eighteen-frame flash. It replaces the previous
Veo-derived cue and sits roughly eight decibels higher. Both picture streams
are copied unchanged; the earlier mix retains its timing and gain.

Further auditions replaced that voice with the refined fifth take: slower,
deeper and more muffled. Final v5 places the exact selected sample at 11.75 seconds
for the eighteen-frame flash. The director accepted this delivery; all 302 picture
frames and the earlier soundtrack remain unchanged from v4.
