# RED TEARS — decisions

## Production decisions in sequence

References to retained comparison edits describe the review period. At closeout,
the final 4K master, original sources, reusable shot assets, voice auditions,
stems and records are retained; superseded complete edits and mixes are removed
after verification. The production record identifies each removed artifact.

- The director selected RED TEARS from the initial workshop and asked to
  simplify it. The severe lighting and white/black/red palette were retained.
- The main transformation uses one jewel, one reversed tear and one eye.
  Movement of the painted tears and additional transformations were removed.
- A display-case latch and a final enclosed breath imply context without
  adding a visible location or dialogue.
- The initial working edit was 12 seconds at 24 fps in 9:16. These are editorial
  decisions inside the director's requested 10–15-second range.
- The portrait opening keyframe is a built-in image-generation result based
  on the supplied mask. Original media and creation evidence remain private.
- The original workshop remains available in [workshop-v1.md](workshop-v1.md).
  Its ENCORE recommendation and alternate endings are historical proposals.
- Selection of this concept is distinct from accepting a finished film.
- The first motion take was rejected for a descending drop and a double-eye
  reveal. The second supplied a stable shell and usable near-eye performance,
  but its bead swelled in place. Both original takes are retained.
- The selected motion is a local composite: isolated ruby and bead material,
  a fixed clean plate, an upward path and the second take's near-eye performance.
  This keeps the intended event legible without adding another transformation.
- Close inspection overruled a clean automated report on the first composite:
  a pale matte edge traveled with the bead. The corrected version isolates the
  red object's silhouette. The superseded render remains available privately.
- The original synthesized soundtrack uses a case latch to suggest context,
  glass friction to carry the upward movement, a short eye accent and enclosed
  air after the cut to black. Native generated audio is preserved but unused.
- The review export is native portrait resolution. Frame inspection and
  computational audio measurements remain distinct from real-time listening,
  artistic approval and finishing authorization.
- After reviewing v1, the director asked for the gem to become a clearer blood
  streak with tactile upward flow; the round bead looked as though it slid.
- The revision keeps a connected tail at the starting point while a red rivulet
  stretches upward. Its base pool shrinks, its thickness changes, wet highlights
  move across it, and a narrow stain remains after the eye opens. This is a local
  surface-flow effect using the retained shell and eye performance.
- A quiet original sticky-pull stem supports the new texture. The glass-friction
  layer is slightly lower; the eye accent, stare, black ending and total length
  retain their timing. Native v1 remains available for comparison.
- The director approved v2 and requested a 4K upscale. A local 2× Lanczos finish
  preserves the approved blood, eye, timing and soundtrack at 2160 × 3840.
  Audio was copied without re-encoding. All 288 video timestamps and all 564
  audio packets match the native cut; decoded audio is identical.
- A final probe caught missing container primaries and transfer tags. A
  metadata-only remux completed BT.709 limited-range tagging. Every decoded
  picture frame remained identical to the inspected 4K candidate; the candidate
  and its failed tag check are retained privately.

- The director requested a post-black coda: a frontal extreme close-up, both eyes
  frightened, with the existing left-side blood residue and a muffled sound
  behind the rigid mask. The black hold loses six frames; the flash lasts
  eighteen frames. Two final black frames prevent playback from freezing on
  the mask, bringing the revision to 302 frames.
- The new angle uses a continuity frame from the approved cut as its reference.
  One new motion take supplies the first three-quarter second and a short
  nonverbal voice. Later reported stain variation falls outside the selection.
- The new voice is filtered into an enclosed whimper and placed at the flash.
  The earlier soundtrack keeps its timing and gain; there is no global remix.

- The tiny final black segment exposed a decode-timestamp gap in the assembled
  container. Explicit presentation timestamps, a regular decode cadence and
  an exact movie timescale corrected it without re-encoding the native images.
  Every retained original frame matches the approved version. Intermediate
  candidates and failed checks remain private; final checks pass.

- The director praised mask continuity but found the closing voice missing.
  A dedicated ElevenLabs sound effect now replaces the subdued Veo-derived
  cue. The selected human vocal burst is tightened to the existing flash,
  with lighter muffling and more vocal presence. The earlier sound is unchanged.
- Native and 4K picture streams are copied without re-encoding. All frame
  counts, presentation timestamps, crop, blood residue and black holds remain
  unchanged. All earlier versions and source sound takes are retained.

- Further voice auditions narrowed the choice to the opening of the sixth
  take and a local refinement of the fifth. The director chose the refined
  fifth take: slightly slower, lower pitched and more muffled.
- Final v5 inserts the exact selected three-quarter-second sample at the
  existing coda, with its audition level and processing preserved. Native and
  4K picture streams remain identical to v4. The earlier soundtrack is unchanged;
  all auditions and preceding full edits were retained through review.

## Release and closeout

- The director accepted final v5, then supplied the published YouTube URL and
  requested post-production closeout. The release uses the final v5 4K master;
  its local checksum does not describe YouTube's transcode.
- The public page confirms the title, description and publication date. The
  canonical description preserves the published wording, credit order and
  hashtags, including the added `#aivideo`. Only trailing whitespace and the
  final newline are normalized for the source file; the exact platform string
  is retained privately.
- Every project clip, including rejected takes and reusable local composites,
  passed the configured library extraction checkpoint. Those model results
  remain distinct from human review and the director's acceptance of the film.
- Archive retention preserves original source copies and their creation evidence.
  Rebuild dependencies and the limits of historical scripts are documented in
  the [handoff](handoff.md). No byte-identical rebuild is claimed.
- Process improvement: audition short vocal cues as a numbered, matched-level
  set before committing to a final mix, then preserve the exact approved sample.
  See the [vocal-stinger field note](../../docs/craft/field-notes/vocal-stinger-auditions.md).
