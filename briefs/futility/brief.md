# Futility

A photorealistic portrait film built around Helene Johnson's complete short poem. Approved cut: 17 seconds, 9:16, 24 fps. A woman waits in a carefully ordered room. The life she is waiting for is audible outside; she gets up and joins it.

## Text

> It is silly —\
> This waiting for love\
> In a parlor.\
> When love is singing up and down the alley\
> Without a collar.

Helene Johnson, “Futility,” published in *Opportunity*, August 1926. The original text is public domain in the United States. Text and publication source: [Literary Ladies Guide](https://www.literaryladiesguide.com/classic-women-authors-poetry/11-poems-by-helene-johnson/). The film uses a newly generated voice performance. The director selected audition A: Alicia, a measured reading of the complete poem.

## Treatment

Present-day neighborhood, a modest brownstone room and an adjoining pedestrian alley. The director authorized the fresh version; the agent followed the proposed present-day setting. Cast a Black adult woman in her thirties. Let ordinary skin, worn wood, cotton, daylight and incidental street behavior carry the image. The camera begins still and close to her private world; the final movement belongs to her.

The selected performance is grounded and lightly wry, with the poem spoken as a coherent thought. Preserve the reader’s natural pauses and pace. The speaker is recognizing her own habit.

## Registry finding

Searches covered alley, street, laughing, window, curtain, dancing, musician and waiting. Twelve candidates received a first visual audition from retained analysis frames. The most plausible street material looked like glossy advertising; the convincing room material retained recognizable staging from earlier films. The neutral curtain insert could be made to fit, but would not strengthen the proposed cut.

After reviewing this recommendation, the director authorized three coherent new shots with “Proceed,” then asked production to continue. Three new reference images, three native video takes and a new voice performance were produced. An additional sound bed replaces unwanted native chatter in the alley shot. Registry use records are incomplete for some historical clips; absence of a recorded use is not proof that a take is unused.
